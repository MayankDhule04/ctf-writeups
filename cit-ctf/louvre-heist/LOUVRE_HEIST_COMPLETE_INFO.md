# Louvre Heist - Complete Information

## The 1911 Mona Lisa Theft (Most Famous)

### Basic Facts
- **Date**: August 21, 1911 (Monday)
- **Thief**: Vincenzo Peruggia (Italian glazier/handyman)
- **Accomplices**: Vincenzo Lancelotti, Pietro Lancelotti (brothers)
- **Item**: Mona Lisa (La Joconde) by Leonardo da Vinci
- **Location**: Salon Carré (Square Room / Room 711)
- **Duration Missing**: 2 years, 4 months
- **Recovery Date**: December 12, 1913
- **Recovery Location**: Florence, Italy (Uffizi Gallery)
- **Returned to Louvre**: January 4, 1914

### The Heist Details
- Peruggia worked at the Louvre installing protective glass cases
- He hid in a closet overnight (Sunday, August 20)
- Early Monday morning, he removed the painting from its case
- Lifted it off 4 iron pegs
- Removed the frame in a stairwell (frame found later)
- Walked out with painting hidden under his smock/coat
- Kept it in his Paris apartment for 2 years
- Attempted to sell it to Uffizi Gallery director Alfredo Geri
- Arrested and served 7 months in prison

### Key People
- **Thief**: Vincenzo Peruggia
- **Accomplices**: Vincenzo & Pietro Lancelotti
- **Louvre Director**: Théophile Homolle (resigned after theft)
- **Buyer Contact**: Alfredo Geri (Uffizi Gallery director)
- **Artist**: Leonardo da Vinci

### Painting Details
- **Name**: Mona Lisa / La Joconde / La Gioconda
- **Artist**: Leonardo da Vinci
- **Dimensions**: 77 cm × 53 cm (30 in × 21 in)
- **Medium**: Oil on poplar wood panel
- **Subject**: Lisa Gherardini
- **Date Created**: c. 1503–1519

### Locations
- **Theft Location**: Salon Carré, Louvre Museum, Paris, France
- **Hidden**: Rue de l'Hôpital-Saint-Louis, Paris (Peruggia's apartment)
- **Recovery**: Florence, Italy
- **Current Location**: Louvre Museum, Paris

### Motive
- Peruggia claimed patriotic motives
- Believed the painting belonged in Italy
- Wanted to return it to its "homeland"

### Financial Details
- Reward offered: 25,000 francs
- Estimated value: Priceless (insured for ~$100 million in 1911 dollars)
